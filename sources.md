## Ancient Primary Sources

These were drawn upon directly (via fragments, secondary transmission, or scholarship) during expansion. All are in the public domain. Their standard scholarly editions are noted where relevant.

### Used in Chapter 1

  - **Herodotus,** ***Histories*** — Book III (Scylax expedition, Indian tribute: 360 talents of gold-dust); Book VII (Indian troops in Xerxes's army; the Battle of Plataea). Standard edition: Godley (Loeb, 1920). \[HANDOFF\]
  - **Ctesias of Cnidus,** ***Indica*** (c. 398 BCE; fragments via Photius, Persica, and Diodorus). Greek physician at the Achaemenid court who gathered Indian knowledge from informants in Susa. Key for the Achaemenid-as-bridge argument. \[HANDOFF\]

### Used in Chapter 2

  - **Nasadiya Sukta (Rigveda 10.129)** — full text cited in expansion; the "not this, not this" epistemological humility passage. Standard translation: Wendy Doniger, *The Rigveda* (Penguin Classics, 1981). \[HANDOFF\]
  - **Brihadaranyaka Upanishad** — Yajnavalkya's *neti, neti* dialogue; the apophatic method. Standard translation: S. Radhakrishnan, *The Principal Upanishads* (HarperCollins India, 1953). \[HANDOFF\]
  - **Anaximander, fragments** — preserved via Simplicius (*Commentary on Aristotle's Physics*) and Hippolytus (*Refutatio*). \[HANDOFF\]
  - **Heraclitus, fragments** — preserved via Diogenes Laërtius and Clement of Alexandria. Edition used: T.M. Robinson, *Heraclitus: Fragments* (University of Toronto Press, 1987). \[HANDOFF\]

### Used in Chapter 3

  - **Megasthenes,** ***Indica*** (c. 300 BCE; fragments via Arrian, Strabo, Diodorus, Pliny). Key passages: Pataliputra dimensions and municipal council (Arrian, *Indika* VIII–X); seven-class schema (Diodorus II.40–42); the bodyguard of armed women and nightly change of rooms (Strabo XV.1.55). Standard compilation: McCrindle, J.W., *Ancient India as Described by Megasthenes and Arrian* (1877; repr. Asian Educational Services, 2000). \[HANDOFF\]
  - **Kautilya (Chanakya),** ***Arthashastra*** (c. 300–150 BCE; date debated). Chancellor of Chandragupta; the treatise on statecraft that provides the Indian corrective to Megasthenes's idealized account. Key books used: Book I (the education and protection of the king); Book III (on slaves and laborers); Book II (state economic administration). Standard translation: L.N. Rangarajan, *Kautilya: The Arthashastra* (Penguin, 1992). \[HANDOFF\]
  - **Strabo,** ***Geography*****, Book XV** (c. 20 BCE) — critical account of Megasthenes, quotes Apollodorus of Artemita. Loeb edition: Jones (1930). \[HANDOFF\]
  - **Arrian,** ***Indika*** (c. 2nd century CE) — most reliable ancient handler of Megasthenes's Pataliputra material. Loeb edition: Brunt (1983). \[HANDOFF\]

### Used in Chapter 5

  - **Paul Bernard,** ***Fouilles d'Aï Khanoum*** — primary excavation reports (DAFA/CNRS, Paris, vols. I–VIII, 1973–1992). Direct basis for the Klearchos inscription text, the silver disk find, and theater dimensions. \[HANDOFF\]

  

## Modern Scholarly Works — New to This Session

These are works not listed among the 10 key sources in the Project Brief and not found in the manuscript's bibliographic handoffs. They were drawn upon conceptually or for specific details during expansion and should be considered for addition to the research corpus.

### Key New Additions

**Cline, Eric H.** ***1177 B.C.: The Year Civilization Collapsed*****. Princeton University Press, 2014.** Used in: Chapter 2, Section II (Bronze Age collapse) The "Club of Great Powers" framing of the Late Bronze Age world system — Egypt, Mycenae, Hatti, Ugarit as interdependent imperial nodes whose collapse was systemic rather than sequential — is drawn substantially from Cline's analysis. His "systems collapse" model (drawing on complexity theory) is the conceptual framework behind the Chapter 2 passage arguing that the collapse was multifactorial and produced the epistemic rupture that enabled Axial Age philosophy. *Recommended for addition to the notebook.*

  

**Seaford, Richard.** ***The Origins of Philosophy in Ancient Greece and India: A Historical Comparison*****. Cambridge University Press, 2020.** Used in: Chapter 2, Section III (the monetization thesis) The 2020 follow-up to *Money and the Early Greek Mind*, cited in the Chapter 2 bibliographic handoff but likely not in the original 138-source corpus (which predates April 2026 curation). Contains the fullest development of Seaford's argument extended to explicitly compare Greek and Indian philosophical origins. *Verify whether this is in the notebook; if not, add.*

  

**Spooner, D.B. / Archaeological Survey of India. Kumrahar Excavation Reports (1912–1915; 1951–1955).** Used in: Chapter 3, Section I (the Mauryan pillared hall) The specific detail of the polished stone columns found at Kumrahar (modern Patna), confirming Megasthenes's account of the palace complex, comes from the ASI excavation record. Most accessible secondary account: Sinha, B.P. *Pataliputra* (Patna, 1970) or Singh, Upinder. *A History of Ancient and Early Medieval India* (Pearson, 2008), ch. 6. *Primary excavation reports may not be in the notebook. Recommend adding Singh, Upinder (2008) as the accessible secondary synthesis.*

## Chapter 18 — The Gospel and the Dharma

*Added: April 2026. Chapter 18 expansion (\~886 → \~3,300 words).*

### New Primary Ancient Sources

  - **Gospel of Thomas** (c. 1st–2nd century CE; Coptic text, Nag Hammadi 1945). The non-canonical sayings gospel central to the argument for an Edessa-centered Syriac Christianity in contact with Indian wisdom traditions. Sayings 3, 54, and 110 cited directly. English translation: Marvin Meyer, *The Gospel of Thomas: The Hidden Sayings of Jesus* (HarperSanFrancisco, 1992).
  - **Majjhima Nikaya** — suttas on *karuṇā* and the *brahmavihāras*. Translation: Bhikkhu Ñāṇamoli and Bhikkhu Bodhi, *The Middle Length Discourses of the Buddha* (Wisdom Publications, 1995). Used for the compassion/Bodhisattva/Sermon on the Mount parallel in Section III.
  - **Nestle-Aland** ***Novum Testamentum Graece***, 28th ed. — Greek text of Matthew 5–7 (Sermon on the Mount); cited for the *ptōchoi tō pneumati* etymology in Section II.

### New Modern Scholarly Works

**Crossan, John Dominic.** ***The Historical Jesus: The Life of a Mediterranean Jewish Peasant*****. HarperSanFrancisco, 1991.** Used in: Ch. 18, Section IV (the teacher who did not perform sacrifice). The social form of the Jesus movement — wandering teacher without institutional authority, inner circle of disciples, outer circle of lay followers, address to the marginalized — is analyzed in its cultural context. Crossan's work provides the basis for the argument that the *śramaṇa* social form is visible in the ministry of Jesus in ways that the prior Jewish tradition does not fully explain. *Likely in the notebook — verify; if not, add.*

  

**Patterson, Stephen J.** ***The Gospel of Thomas and Jesus*****. Polebridge Press, 1993.** Used in: Ch. 18, Section V (the Thomas community and the eastern edge). The most rigorous scholarly treatment of the Thomas tradition, its relationship to the Synoptics, and its affinities with Gnostic wisdom traditions. Central to the Edessa → Buddhist-contact argument. *Verify whether in notebook; recommend adding if not.*

  

**McEvilley, Thomas.** ***The Shape of Ancient Thought*****. Allworth Press, 2002. — Chapters 29–32.** Previously listed in sources.md for Chs. 1–5 (chapters 25–28 cited there). Chapter 18 draws on chapters 29–32 specifically: Buddhist-Christian ethical parallels and the channels of possible transmission. The primary scholarly frame for the whole chapter. *No new addition needed — McEvilley is already in the notebook. Note extended chapter range used.*

  

**Eliot, Charles.** ***Hinduism and Buddhism: An Historical Sketch*****. 3 vols. Routledge, 1921.** Used in: Ch. 18 (general comparative framework). Vol. III contains a chapter on Buddhist influence in the Mediterranean world with a useful compilation of parallel passages between Buddhist suttas and Christian texts. An older work but still a reliable assembly of primary-text parallels. *Likely not in the notebook (pre-modern scholarship). Low priority for addition.*

  

**Gruber, Elmar R. and Holger Kersten.** ***The Original Jesus: The Buddhist Sources of Christianity*****. Element Books, 1995.** Used in: Ch. 18 (evidence compilation, Section VI). Advances a stronger transmission claim than the chapter endorses. Cited for its evidence-gathering rather than its conclusions, which the chapter explicitly flags as overreaching. Useful as a bibliography of parallel passages. *Probably not in the notebook. Low priority — used only as an evidence catalogue, not as a scholarly authority.*
